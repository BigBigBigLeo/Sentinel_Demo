# Sentinel Evidence Base

This note captures two papers that are directly useful for Sentinel's product thesis, market-entry logic, and pilot design. The goal is not to inflate claims, but to anchor them in evidence and also keep the boundary conditions explicit.

## Why These Papers Matter

Sentinel is making two linked claims:

- precision and decision technologies can create real economic and environmental value
- adoption does not happen automatically just because the technology exists

The two papers below map well to those two claims.

## Paper 1

**Barnes et al. (2019)**

Exploring the adoption of precision agricultural technologies: A cross regional study of EU farmers.
Land Use Policy, 80, 163-174.

Local source used in this repo workflow:
- `BusinessPlan/References/Papers/Barnes et al 2019 - Exploring the adoption of precision agricultural technologies.pdf`

### What It Studied

- 971 arable crop growers
- 5 European countries: Belgium, Germany, Greece, the Netherlands, and the UK
- compared non-adopters, machine-guidance adopters, and more advanced adopters using variable-rate nitrogen technology bundles

### Findings We Should Keep

- adoption is strongly constrained by entry cost, farm size, and income
- perceived payback matters: farmers who believe the technology will pay back are more likely to adopt it
- moving from simpler guidance tools to more information-intensive tools depends more on advisory support and information-seeking behavior
- subsidy and taxation were seen as positive adoption drivers
- indirect support also matters: demonstration of real benefits and training can be as important as direct financial incentives
- the paper explicitly warns that evidence on economic and environmental benefits is uneven in real field conditions, so promotion should not rely on generic claims alone

### What This Means for Sentinel

For Sentinel, this paper is especially important for go-to-market in the EU and the Netherlands:

- our bottleneck is not only model capability; it is trust in payback
- demo, advisory credibility, and pilot proof matter as much as product features
- StartLife, WUR, pilot partners, and local expert networks are not side channels; they are part of adoption infrastructure
- we should avoid a one-size-fits-all rollout story and instead start with growers or partners who already have the operational capacity to use decision support well
- training, demonstration, and evidence-backed ROI storytelling should be treated as product-adjacent assets

### Best Sentinel Use Cases for This Citation

- EU market-entry narrative
- Dutch pilot and accelerator applications
- explaining why we need validation, not just more engineering
- framing demo + advisory + audit as a full adoption stack

## Paper 2

**Lan and Ban (2025)**

The Farm-Level Economic and Environmental Benefits of Precision Agriculture Technology Adoption: A Meta-Analysis of Global Evidence.
Sustainability, 17(24), 11223.

Source:
- https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/17/24/11223

### What It Studied

- meta-analysis of 85 empirical studies
- 1472 independent farm observations worldwide
- evaluated both economic and environmental effects of precision agriculture technology adoption

### Findings We Should Keep

The article reports that, on average, PAT adoption was associated with:

- ROI increase of 22.3%
- net profit increase of 18.5%
- nitrogen use efficiency increase of 15.1%
- pesticide application reduction of 12.8%
- greenhouse gas emissions reduction of 9.4%

The paper also shows strong heterogeneity:

- variable-rate technology and auto-guidance show the clearest benefits
- benefits are stronger in large-scale grain systems
- benefits are weaker and less stable in small farms and developing-country settings
- technology type, farm size, region, and development level all materially change outcomes

### What This Means for Sentinel

This paper is useful because it supports Sentinel's value proposition, but only if we preserve the context dependence:

- it supports the claim that precision decision systems can create both economic and environmental upside
- it also tells us not to oversell average results as universal results
- Sentinel should position itself as a context-aware decision OS, not as a generic precision-agriculture silver bullet
- pilot design should concentrate on scenarios where decision leverage is high and variability is costly, such as high-value crops, protected environments, and operations with meaningful quality or compliance sensitivity
- our product narrative should emphasize better targeting, input efficiency, and auditable reasoning, while being explicit that realized gains depend on crop, scale, infrastructure, and operator workflow

### Best Sentinel Use Cases for This Citation

- support for the V5 input-efficiency and sustainability narrative
- investor and grant materials that need quantitative external evidence
- pilot design and KPI selection
- guardrails for claim language in marketing and fundraising materials

## Combined Strategic Reading

Taken together, these papers give a clean external logic for Sentinel:

- Barnes et al. explains why adoption is hard in Europe: trust, payback confidence, advisory channels, and demonstration matter
- Lan and Ban shows why the category remains important: when precision technologies fit the context, the upside can be economically and environmentally meaningful

For Sentinel, the combined implication is clear:

- do not lead with abstract AI capability
- lead with demonstrable decision value in the right context
- pair software with proof, workflow integration, and local validation

## How To Apply This in Sentinel Materials

### Product Narrative

Use the literature to support a bounded claim:

- Sentinel improves the quality, speed, and auditability of agronomic decision making
- in the right operating context, better targeting can support both input efficiency and economic performance
- actual gains are context dependent and must be validated through pilots

### Market-Entry Narrative

Use Barnes et al. to justify why the current Dutch route is logical:

- StartLife and WUR are not just funding channels; they are trust-building and validation channels
- pilots, expert support, and demonstration are part of adoption economics

### Pilot Design Narrative

Use the meta-analysis to shape pilot KPIs:

- decision latency reduction
- input-use reduction
- avoided quality downgrade
- yield or grade protection
- nitrogen-use efficiency or chemical-use efficiency
- auditability and intervention traceability

### Claim Discipline

Do not cite these papers as proof that Sentinel itself has already produced these outcomes.
Use them as category-level support and as justification for why pilot measurement matters.
